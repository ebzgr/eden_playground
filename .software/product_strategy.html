Marketing for Betterment · Product Strategy

Eden Playground

Audience: Product, Strategy, Co-founders Last reviewed: April 2026 Status: Pre-build · Planning phase

The strategic overview for Eden Playground — what it is, who it's for, why it matters, how it works, what success looks like, and the open decisions that still need answers. This is the document you read before making any product decision.

01 The problem we exist to solve

Manipulative marketing is not on the fringe — it is the default. Artificial urgency, manufactured social proof, dark patterns, guilt nudges, gamified compulsion loops. Brands and platforms spend billions refining these techniques. Most people encounter them dozens of times a day and have no vocabulary, no mental model, and no defences.

The standard response — policy papers, academic warnings, regulatory action — operates too far from the individual to change behaviour. You cannot resist what you cannot name. And you cannot name what you have never been shown.

The asymmetry
Billions vs. nothing
Brands have decades of optimised persuasion science. Consumers have occasional news articles. There is no countervailing infrastructure — no platform built for the other side.
Why education fails
Knowing is not enough
Research consistently shows that being told about a manipulation technique does not protect you from it. Experience-based learning — feeling it, then understanding it — creates much more durable immunity.
The research gap
No controlled ground
Science on persuasion and awareness largely relies on surveys or observational data. There is no consented, controlled platform built to run causal experiments on what actually makes awareness stick.
◈ Why now: Awareness of manipulative design is rising — regulation is moving (DSA, dark patterns legislation), public sentiment is shifting, and there is a growing community of advocates, researchers, and designers who want something to point people to. The window to become the canonical platform for this is open. It will not stay open indefinitely.

02 What we are building

Eden Playground is an immersive awareness platform. Users are invited on a journey through a series of interactive Worlds — each one a narrative environment built around a real cluster of manipulative marketing techniques. Users experience the techniques directly, then receive a debrief that names and explains what just happened to them.

The result is not just awareness within the platform. The interventions are designed as portable mental models — vocabulary and pattern-recognition that users carry into their daily digital lives and apply in real commercial environments.

→ The one-line pitch: We let people experience manipulation in a safe environment so they can recognise and resist it everywhere else.

Three things this product does simultaneously

For users
Raise individual awareness
Immersive experience creates lasting pattern-recognition. Not a lecture — a memory. Users leave knowing the name of what was done to them, how it works, and how to spot it in the wild.
For society
Create market pressure
When enough consumers can name a manipulative design pattern, it loses competitive value. Informed users erode the ROI of manipulation, which creates incentives for ethical design — without waiting for regulation.
For science
Advance research
A consented, modular, randomised experiment platform that commercial platforms will never allow external researchers to build. Studies what works, who it affects most, and what actually builds durable immunity.

These three goals are not in tension — they are the same product. The research rigor makes the awareness claims credible. The awareness mission keeps the research ethically grounded. The social demand goal is what scales both.

03 Who we are building it for

The platform has a clear two-phase user strategy, with students as the confirmed first cohort and the general public as the growth phase. These are not the same user — and designing for one without being clear about the other creates drift.

Phase 1 user — the marketing-aware student

Business and marketing students, recruited through the university. They are not naive about manipulation — they have studied it in class. That is precisely the point. Knowing the name of a technique does not protect you from it. These users will discover, firsthand, that their academic knowledge is not immunity. That realisation is the hook, and it is uniquely powerful for this cohort.

A digitally literate adult who has a vague, nagging sense that something is being done to them online — but lacks the vocabulary to name it. Less analytically primed than the student cohort. More emotionally driven. They are not looking for research findings; they are looking for a feeling of agency returned to them.

Academics, NGO analysts, policymakers. They do not go through the Worlds — they use the experiment data and read the dissemination outputs. The founder's own research agenda is the first instance of this user: the platform is simultaneously a product and a research infrastructure for academic publication.

04 How the experience works

The experience is a Journey — a sequenced series of Worlds, wrapped in an overarching narrative that makes completing the whole thing feel meaningful. The journey has a beginning, a climax, and an ending. Completing it means something.

The journey arc

1
Invitation. User is called into the world by Gaia — a mythic personification of the natural order that commercial manipulation is slowly eroding. The invitation sets the stakes and the emotional register: this is a quest, not a tutorial.
2
Consent. Before any data is saved, users understand what they're entering — that they will encounter real manipulative techniques, that content may be algorithmically adapted, that they are pseudonymous. Consent is woven into the narrative, not bolted on.
3
World play. Each World is a self-contained narrative environment built around a technique cluster. Users make real decisions within it. The techniques are live — not described, but experienced. The user does not know the debrief is coming.
4
Debrief. The World reveals what just happened. Technique named. Mechanism explained. Real-world parallel given. The debrief is part of the narrative — not a separate screen. Users leave with a mental model, not just a feeling.
5
Token earned. Completing the World's mission earns its token — an orb in the mythic theme, a resource in the sci-fi theme. Tokens are the tangible record of what the user has understood and survived.
6
Shield of Eden. Collecting all tokens unlocks the Shield of Eden. The user is recognised as an ambassador — someone who has faced the manipulation and can name it. They can now bring others in.

Narrative themes — the same journey, different skin

The entire journey can be experienced through different narrative themes. The underlying structure — Worlds, missions, tokens, Shield — never changes. Only the aesthetic and character layer changes. This is a research variable, not just a design choice. Different themes may produce different completion rates, different emotional responses, different awareness outcomes.

Theme 1 — MVP1
Mythic · Gaia's quest
Gaia calls the user as her Knight. Tokens are orbs of wisdom, vision, patience. The Shield of Eden grants the title Knight of Eden. Letters from Gaia arrive between Worlds. Tone: epic, hopeful, earthy.
Theme 2 — Post-MVP
Sci-fi · Space voyage
User travels between planets as an intergalactic ambassador. Resources gathered per World. The Galaxy Federation issues missions. Tone: curious, expansive, cool. Same mechanics — different universe.
Theme 3+ — Future
To be designed
The architecture supports any number of additional themes without altering World content or research logic. New themes can be added as the platform grows.
✓ MVP1 scope confirmed: Mythic · Gaia's Quest is the only theme for MVP1. Sci-fi and all further themes are post-MVP decisions. The underlying structure — Worlds, missions, tokens, Shield — is built once and theme-agnostic; adding themes later requires no structural rebuild.

Design principles — how we engage without manipulating

This product studies manipulation. It will not use it. The engagement model is built on four principles that are the deliberate opposite of the techniques we study:

Principle 1
Curiosity, not urgency
No countdown timers. No artificial scarcity. No "last chance" copy. Users move at their own pace. The draw is the desire to understand — not the fear of missing out.
Principle 2
Empowerment, not guilt
No shame for being manipulated. No guilt about past behaviour. The tone is consistently: "now you know, you are stronger for it." Competence grows; self-blame doesn't.
Principle 3
Wit, not fear
Irony and humour are primary tools. Making the familiar feel strange is more powerful than making it feel dangerous. When a dark pattern feels absurd, it loses its grip.
Principle 4
Narrative, not lectures
Debriefs are part of the story, not separate screens. Understanding arrives as revelation, not instruction. Users remember what they felt — not what they were told.

05 The Worlds

Each World is a self-contained narrative environment built around one primary cluster of manipulative marketing techniques. Together they form the full journey. Below is the current set, with a provisional prioritisation — this order should be revisited once the target user profile is confirmed and acquisition channels are known.

WorldWhat users encounterWhat they leave knowingPriority
Gaia Invitation The opening narrative — how the platform presents itself, sets stakes, and earns consent Why this journey exists and what they are agreeing to P0 — must go first
Deal World Pricing psychology: anchoring, decoy pricing, discount framing, the illusion of a bargain How price presentation manipulates perceived value, regardless of the actual number P1
Urgency World Artificial scarcity, countdown timers, "act now" copy, manufactured FOMO How time pressure is fabricated and how to recognise when a deadline isn't real P1
Social World Fake consensus, fabricated testimonials, follower counts, bandwagon mechanics How popularity signals are constructed and what questions to ask before trusting them P2
Gamification World Streak mechanics, progress bars, variable reward loops, achievement systems How game-like structures are used to create compulsion without conscious consent P2

→ Prioritisation rationale: Deal and Urgency are the techniques users encounter most frequently and feel most viscerally. They are also the cleanest for first experiments — the behavioural signals are clear and the debrief writes itself. P2 Worlds require the platform to be proven before we invest in their design.

Every World follows a shared structure: a narrative mission, embedded marketing interventions, a completion condition, a debrief, and a token. The content varies — the architecture is identical. This modularity is what makes the research possible.

06 The research mission

Eden Playground is not just a product. It is the only consented, controlled, experience-based research platform for studying manipulative marketing and its countermeasures. That is a genuine scientific contribution — and a significant competitive moat.

Two research directions that feed each other

Direction 1
How techniques evolve and who they affect
Which manipulative techniques become more effective over time? Which user segments are most vulnerable? What environmental factors amplify or dampen their effect? This is the "understand the enemy" track.
Direction 2
What builds lasting awareness
Which awareness interventions actually work? Does narrative framing matter? Does timing of debrief change retention? Which mental models persist? This is the "build the defence" track.

These two directions are not competing. Insights from Direction 1 (how techniques work on whom) directly inform the design of Direction 2 interventions (who needs what kind of awareness support). The platform is the infrastructure for both.

What makes this research credible

  • Random assignment — users are assigned to versions of a World, not self-selected. Causal claims are defensible.
  • Pre-registered hypotheses — experiments are defined before data is collected. No p-hacking or retrospective framing.
  • Heterogeneous effect analysis — results broken down by user segment. We are not optimising for the average; we are finding who is most affected.
  • Guardrail metrics — experiments can be stopped if emotional safety thresholds are crossed. Participant wellbeing is a live instrument, not an afterthought.
  • Public dissemination — every experiment produces a plain-language summary: what we tested, what we found, what it means.

Ethical design as a competitive advantage

The ethics framework is not a constraint on the research — it is what makes the research trustworthy. We study manipulation without amplifying it. We collect data without identifying users. We run experiments without deceiving participants about the nature of the experience. This is the thing commercial platforms cannot credibly claim.

✓ Non-negotiables (these do not get traded off for speed or scope): No PII or email required. Informed consent before any data is stored. Technique intensity never exceeds real-world commercial norms. Every content version passes human review before users see it. Debrief is built into every technique encounter — always.

The MVP success condition

Before we set annual targets, we need a proof-of-concept moment: a real user cohort going through at least one World, generating valid experiment data. That is the minimum to confirm the model works. Everything else — additional Worlds, themes, scale — follows only after this is proven.

✓ MVP condition: 50 users complete Gaia Invitation + 1 content World. Experiment arms assigned. Debrief comprehension measured. Enough signal to run a basic analysis. This is the gate to Phase 2.

Things we must not break (guardrail metrics)

  • User distress rate — sessions triggering the wellbeing check must stay below a defined threshold
  • Consent completion — 100% of users must complete informed consent before any data is stored, always
  • Technique intensity — no World may exceed the intensity users encounter on normal commercial platforms
  • Debrief reach — every user who encounters a technique must receive the debrief, with no exceptions

08 Market position and differentiation

There is no direct competitor. That is an opportunity — and a risk. It means we are building a category, not entering one. Category creation requires more activation energy but produces more durable positions.

What existsWhy it falls shortWhat we do differently
Academic research on persuasion Published in journals that consumers never read. No product. No behaviour change. We translate research into lived experience. Findings become debriefs. Science becomes awareness.
Media literacy programmes Curriculum-based, lecture-led, school-age focused. Doesn't reach adults in their context. Experience-based, narrative-led, self-directed. Adults choose to enter. No syllabus smell.
Dark patterns awareness content Articles, Twitter threads, browser extensions. Raises awareness in the abstract. Not embodied. You feel the pattern before you name it. The debrief lands because the experience is real.

→ Our position: The place where awareness becomes embodied. Not content about manipulation — an experience of it. Not a classroom — a world you enter. Not research for researchers — research that reaches the people being studied.

09 How we reach users

The go-to-market strategy is sequenced in two distinct phases: a controlled academic launch that validates the model, followed by a public growth phase seeded by the ambassador flywheel. Phase 1 is confirmed. Phase 2 is designed but not yet activated.

Phase 1 — University launch (confirmed)

The first user cohort is recruited through the academic institution. This is not a compromise channel — it is strategically ideal for Phase 1. Students arrive understanding research participation. The ethics review process is familiar. The consent model fits cleanly. And word of mouth within a student cohort is fast, horizontal, and trust-based.

✓ Confirmed channel: University student recruitment via the academic institution. This activates the ethics committee pathway, the academic consent model, and the institutional credibility that makes the research publishable.

The strategic risk of starting with students is sample homogeneity — they are younger, more analytically oriented, and more marketing-literate than the eventual public user. This must be documented as a limitation in research outputs. It is not a reason to delay; it is a reason to design Phase 2 as a deliberate extension, not an assumption that the same experience will land the same way.

Phase 2 — Public growth (designed, not yet activated)

Peer-to-peer recruitment is the most mission-aligned growth mechanism that exists — users who completed the journey bring in people they trust. Only compounds after completion rates are meaningful. The ambassador model — the long-term growth engine

The Shield of Eden is not just a reward mechanic. It is the growth model. A user who earns the Shield has a story — they faced the manipulation, understood it, and completed the journey. That is a social credential. When they bring someone else in, it arrives with peer trust and narrative context that no paid channel can replicate.

This flywheel is what makes the product self-sustaining over time. But it only starts spinning when completion rates are real. Phase 1 seeds it. Phase 2 uses it.
