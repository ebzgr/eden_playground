Worlds

Worlds are the playground’s modular stages. Each one focuses on a coherent theme of marketing techniques while staying honest to how real products blend multiple levers — and each one sits inside a larger journey whose narrative theme can change across versions.

Journey, narrative themes, and versioning

Users do not hop between disconnected mini-games; they follow a journey that strings Worlds together. The overarching narrative can take different themes — mythic, sci-fi, or others — and World-level presentation (characters, copy, visuals) shifts with that theme. The same underlying structure (missions, tokens, completion) can therefore appear as a hero’s quest with Gaia and orbs, or as a sci-fi voyage between planets to power the Shield, or as future themes we add later.

Versioning encodes these choices: a “version” of the experience can mean a specific narrative theme, a set of World variants, or updated content within a World. Users are assigned to versions for fair comparison in research — see Research Methodology for random assignment and experiment workflow.

🎭 Why it matters: Themes make the journey memorable and emotionally engaging; versioning lets us test whether a mythic frame, a sci-fi frame, or another frame changes awareness, completion, or trust — without conflating those effects with the marketing techniques inside each World.

What defines a World

Each World revolves around a primary marketing theme — for example pricing, urgency, social proof, or gamification. The World’s design makes that theme legible: users see how a class of techniques shows up in a storyline or challenge.

In real marketing, techniques are combined. So a World may include elements associated with other Worlds (e.g. gamification touches inside a pricing World) as long as the primary learning focus stays clear. That mirrors how persuasion works in the wild and keeps the experience credible.

In-world narrative: puzzle, story, and interventions

The narrative of each World is built around a challenge — a puzzle to solve, a storyline to follow, or a sequence of decisions — during which users encounter marketing interventions embedded in context. Our goal is awareness through personalised, experience-driven learning: users feel the technique, then understand it, rather than reading a dry definition first.

  • Marketing interventions are the persuasive patterns we surface inside the World’s scene (e.g. decoy pricing, streak rewards, scarcity copy).
  • Personalisation (where ethically and technically appropriate) tailors difficulty, pacing, or framing so we can study heterogeneous effects — who benefits most from which awareness path.

Cross-cutting narrative interventions

Beyond what happens inside a World’s scene, users may receive other kinds of interventions that belong to the overall journey, not to a single World’s mechanic. Examples:

  • A letter from Gaia between Worlds.
  • A video message from a fictional “Galaxy Federation” in a sci-fi-themed run.
  • Similar devices in other themes — always aligned with consent, ethics, and intensity boundaries.

These interventions support the story and give us a way to study different intervention formats (in-world vs. meta-narrative, text vs. video, character vs. abstract) and their effectiveness for awareness. They connect to the broader framework in Interventions.

Missions, tokens, and the Shield of Eden

Each World sets a mission: finish the challenge, meet the success criteria, and earn that World’s final token (in a mythic theme, these may appear as orbs — e.g. wisdom, vision, patience — with the same structural role). Completing the full journey — collecting all tokens — unlocks the final gift: early access to the Shield of Eden and recognition as an ambassador. That closure rewards persistence and ties the narrative arc to the project’s mission.

For metrics and how completion events are measured, see Metrics & Measurement when that page is populated.

Versioning and user assignment

When a user enters the playground or a specific World, they are assigned a version according to the active experiment or rollout. Versions may differ by narrative theme, by intervention set, or by incremental content changes. Behaviour and responses are tracked so we can compare versions and improve both awareness outcomes and experience quality.

For the full picture on ethics, consent, and data handling, see Ethics & Data Protection. For the platform concept and journey framing, see Conceptual Layer.
