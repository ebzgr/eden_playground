Intervention and social effectiveness idea

Importance: Medium  ·  Status: Open

A two-stage world that connects marketing exposure (in a market) to measurable societal outcomes (in a cooperative fight). Research goal: test whether different marketing strategies (e.g., manipulative price framing, urgency pressure) spill over into cooperation, trust, and equity aversion.

Ideas

  • Stage 1 — The Market (intervention happens here): The player enters a colorful market to buy gear before a demon fight. The market contains different “shop styles” that implement experimental conditions: price framing tricks, anchor pricing, “limited time” banners, decoy bundles, etc. The player’s loadout is real (it affects their performance and options later), so the market decisions feel meaningful.
  • Stage 2 — The Fight (measurement happens here): Through collaborative tasks, the player and others fight the demon and earn mana points. During the fight stage, measure societal outcomes:
    • Resource sharing before the war: “What resources will you bring for the team?” (measure cooperation/trust trade-offs).
    • Ultimatum game after the fight: ask users to split gathered points with another player (measure equity aversion / fairness preferences).
  • Narrative loop: Market → gear-up → demon fight → mana earned → mana unlocks the next orb. The orb feels earned through both combat and character: “You didn’t just win — you learned how persuasion can change how we treat each other.”
  • Research payoff: This world creates a clean structure for publishable findings: marketing manipulation (controlled in market) → downstream cooperation/trust/fairness behavior (measured in fight). This supports papers on cross-domain spillovers of marketing tactics into societal outcomes.
  • Ending / debrief idea (open): After the demon is defeated, show a playful “spellbook debrief” that reveals which market tactics were present and how they tend to affect decisions — without shaming the player. Optionally, tie a final choice (“Share the last mana potion?”) to a memorable closing beat.
