Ethics & Data Protection

This page describes how we protect users, design interventions responsibly, and run experiments fairly. It complements the ethical guardrails in the Conceptual Layer and the procedures in Research Methodology. Further detail (e.g. region-specific privacy law, full consent copy, and review checklists) can be added as the platform matures.

Privacy

We treat privacy as a design requirement, not an afterthought (privacy by design). The goal is to run rigorous research with the minimum data necessary and without building a conventional “account” around real-world identity.

What we do not collect for identity

We do not require or store identifiers such as email addresses, phone numbers, or real names for users to use the playground. We avoid collecting personally identifiable information (PII) for profiling or marketing in the commercial sense.

Return key and pseudonymous identifier

Users who wish to come back later and continue the narrative can download a key (or equivalent credential) generated for them. That key is the primary pseudonymous identifier our systems use to recognise a returning session and to link behaviour and research data to a stable random ID — not to a person’s legal identity. Users should store the key safely; without it, continuity may not be recoverable by design, which limits re-identification risk from our side.

  • Data minimisation: Store only what the research and product experience require; default to aggregation where possible.
  • Purpose limitation: Use data only for stated research, safety, and improvement purposes.
  • Security: Protect stored data and keys in transit and at rest according to current best practices.
  • Transparency: Clear explanations at the point of use about what is recorded and why.
🔐 Region-specific obligations (for example GDPR, where applicable) will be reflected in consent wording, retention limits, and user rights — documented alongside implementation, not only in this wiki.

Emotional manipulation and participant safety

The playground studies manipulative marketing — which means we must be especially careful not to reproduce harm under the guise of research. We aim to keep interventions within safe intensity, aligned with the principle that we mirror common commercial patterns without escalating beyond what users might ordinarily encounter, while still making patterns visible for learning.

Safe interventions

Content and experiments go through human review before reaching users. We screen for undue distress, targeting of vulnerable groups without safeguards, and scenarios that would cross our intensity boundaries. Adaptive or automated systems that propose variants remain under human oversight so unsafe combinations are not deployed.

Metrics for well-being, society, and the planet

A core part of our mission is to improve not only commercial awareness but outcomes that matter beyond clicks. We therefore continuously develop and refine measurement related to mental health, societal impact, and planetary well-being — working with appropriate expertise where needed — so we can detect negative effects early and reward designs that genuinely help users and the public good.

Debriefing and understanding user status

Debriefing is built into the experience by design: after meaningful encounters, users receive explanations of what techniques appeared, why they matter, and how similar patterns show up online. In addition, we use carefully designed questions (surveys or in-flow prompts) to understand users’ status — for example stress, clarity, or perceived pressure — in ethical, voluntary ways. These inputs support both user reflection and our evaluation of whether interventions remain acceptable over time.

More on intervention formats appears under Interventions; metric definitions will live under Metrics & Measurement as they are specified.

Unbiased design and fair evaluation

In both product design and experimentation, we strive for unbiased evaluation: comparisons should reflect real differences in interventions or narratives, not hidden skew from measurement, assignment, or reporting. That includes pre-registered hypotheses where appropriate, transparent assignment (e.g. randomisation — see Research Methodology), guarding against selective reporting, and analysing heterogeneous effects so we do not optimise only for a narrow subgroup by default.

Measures to reduce legal risk (defamation, trademarks, and related issues)

We educate about manipulative patterns; we do not set out to attack identifiable real companies or individuals. The following practices reduce exposure to claims such as defamation, trademark infringement, or unfair competition, while preserving pedagogical clarity. They are operational commitments, not a substitute for jurisdiction-specific legal advice — we involve legal review when content approaches real-world brands or sensitive allegations.

  • Fictional or composite brands: Prefer clearly fictional companies, products, and characters when illustrating techniques, unless we have a clear, reviewed basis for referencing real entities.
  • No false statements of fact: Avoid asserting that a specific real company engaged in wrongdoing; focus on generic patterns and educational framing.
  • Trademark discipline: Do not use third-party logos, names, or trade dress in ways that suggest endorsement or origin; use generic or original assets; obtain clearance or licence where a real mark must be discussed for research transparency.
  • Satire and clarity: Where irony or pastiche is used, make the fictional or illustrative nature obvious to reasonable users so intent is educational, not misleading.
  • Editorial and legal review: Maintain a checklist for releases that mention real sectors or brands; escalate borderline content for legal sign-off.
  • Documentation: Keep records of design rationale and approvals for high-visibility or sensitive scenarios.
⚖️ These measures aim to align creative and research goals with lower litigation risk. They do not guarantee outcomes in every jurisdiction; formal policies and counsel should track the product as it scales.
