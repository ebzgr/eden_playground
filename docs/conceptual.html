Conceptual Layer

Mission & Purpose

Marketing for Betterment's playground is a core initiative under the Marketing for Betterment project. Its mission is threefold:

  • Raise awareness. Help individuals recognise and understand the manipulative marketing techniques they encounter in everyday digital environments. We seek to understand what methods of awareness-building are most effective — what actually helps people get manipulated less and gives them a lasting understanding of the techniques being used on them.
  • Create demand for social responsibility. By making manipulation visible and giving people the tools to recognise it, we intend to create public demand for more responsible marketing practices. When consumers can identify manipulative design, the competitive advantage of using it erodes — companies are incentivised to adopt transparent, ethical approaches instead.
  • Advance scientific research. The playground provides a controlled, modular research environment where hypotheses about persuasion, awareness, and adaptive optimisation can be tested rigorously. It fills a critical gap: most commercial platforms do not allow external researchers to run experiments, observe design variations, or access behavioural data. This platform does.
⚠️ All research conducted here is carried out through the lens of consumer, societal, and planetary well-being. This is a foundational principle of the Marketing for Betterment initiative. Even questions about how marketing techniques evolve and become more effective are pursued for awareness and public benefit — never for for-profit business optimisation.

Platform Concept

Journey, worlds, and narrative themes

The overall experience is a journey: users move through a sequence of interactive modules called Worlds, each with its own mission and role in the arc. The whole narrative can take different themes (fantasy, sci-fi, mythic guide, and others); how each World looks, sounds, and speaks shifts with the active theme. Those narrative and theme choices are reflected in our versioning — different versions of the journey can run in parallel for research, and users may be assigned to a specific narrative strand so we can compare outcomes fairly.

Missions, tokens, and completion

The journey invites users to visit Worlds in order, complete the mission in each, and earn that World’s final token. Users who collect all tokens receive the final gift: early access to the Shield of Eden and recognition as an ambassador of the playground — a visible milestone that rewards completion and signals commitment to the project’s mission.

🌍 Example — mythic theme: The user is invited by Gaia, a divine personification of Earth, on a hero’s journey. On the landing page they meet Gaia; after a short exchange, they are sent through Worlds to gather orbs (e.g. orb of wisdom, orb of vision, orb of patience). Collecting all orbs, they become the knight of Gaia and receive the Shield of Eden.
🚀 Example — sci-fi theme: The same structural journey can appear as a space voyage: travelling between planets to gather resources that activate the Shield, becoming Gaia’s intergalactic ambassador. The mechanics (missions, tokens, completion) stay aligned; only the narrative skin changes.

Worlds and research

Each World centres on a coherent cluster of marketing techniques (for example pricing psychology or gamification), presented through story, puzzles, and interventions — but the real world of marketing mixes levers, so a World may still contain elements that echo other techniques. Worlds are modular and adaptive: new Worlds can be added as techniques evolve, and each World evolves through versioning; users are assigned versions so we can measure what works and for whom.

For how Worlds are defined, how themes map to versions, and how in-world and cross-cutting interventions fit together, see Worlds and Research Methodology.

Research Directions

The playground pursues two complementary research directions:

I. Understanding How Optimised Marketing Techniques Evolve

We study how marketing techniques that undergo adaptive optimisation change over time. Which approaches become more effective? Which stagnate or decline? How do different techniques escalate or converge? A particular focus is on the heterogeneity of effects — understanding that the same technique can affect different user segments very differently. Some individuals may be more susceptible, while others may be resistant. Identifying these differences is essential for understanding who is most harmed by optimised manipulation.

II. Creating Awareness and Reducing Effectiveness of Manipulation

We investigate how to create effective awareness through immersive experiences — stories, games, and scenario-based interactions. By designing different versions of awareness interventions and continually improving them through adaptive testing, we work toward understanding how to optimise platform content so that manipulative techniques become less effective on informed users.

🔬 Even in the first research direction, our goal is fundamentally educational. We study how manipulation evolves not to make it more powerful, but to understand it well enough to build effective countermeasures and inform policy. This is research for awareness, not for profit.

For implementation details on how adaptive testing is conducted, including metrics, variables, and the research workflow, see Research Methodology.

Design Philosophy & Principles

Playground, Not Classroom

The experience is designed as a playground, not a classroom. Users learn through immersion, exploration, and emotional connection — not through lectures or abstract definitions. Research shows that simply knowing about a manipulative technique does not protect people from it. Awareness delivered as information remains disconnected from everyday life. We invite people to experience influence directly, making it intuitive and memorable.

Art and Creativity Over Fear and Guilt

Unlike the very techniques we study, we do not use urgency, fear, guilt, or shame to grab attention. Instead, we rely on art and creativity as our primary engagement instruments. We motivate users through:

  • Curiosity — making people want to explore and discover.
  • Empowerment — giving users a sense of growing competence and control.
  • Humor — using wit and irony to make the experience enjoyable and shareable.
  • Storytelling — using narrative to create emotional connection and memorability.

Breaking the Normality

One of the deepest challenges in combating manipulative marketing is that many harmful techniques — confirmshaming, artificial urgency, guilt-based nudges — have become so commonplace that they are accepted as normal. Users do not question them because they encounter them everywhere. We use art and creativity to break this normality. By dramatizing and personifying these techniques, we make the invisible visible and the normalised feel strange again. When something feels strange, people start to question it.

Interventive Messaging

Throughout the experience, users receive interventive messages — brief, contextual insights that help them understand what just happened and why. These messages serve a dual purpose: they support better decision-making within the platform, and they act as portable interventions — mental models and heuristics that users can carry into their daily digital lives.

For details on how interventions are designed and versioned, see Interventions.

Ethical Guardrails

Because this platform intentionally exposes users to real manipulative techniques for research and educational purposes, we hold ourselves to strict ethical standards:

  • Privacy by design: We do not require email addresses or similar conventional PII. Users may use a downloaded return key as a pseudonymous identifier; research data is tied to that key, not to legal identity. See Ethics & Data Protection.
  • Informed consent is obtained before any data is saved. Users know they are interacting with a research platform.
  • Pre-notification: Users are informed beforehand that they may encounter persuasive design elements and that some content may be algorithmically adapted.
  • Debriefing is built into the narrative. After encountering techniques, users receive explanations of what was used, why, and how similar techniques appear in real digital environments.
  • Human review is mandatory for every content version before it is served to users, ensuring nothing is harmful to sensitive groups.
  • Intensity boundaries: Manipulative techniques never exceed what users would ordinarily encounter on common commercial platforms. We mirror reality; we do not amplify it.

For the full ethics and data protection framework, see Ethics & Data Protection.

Agent-First Development Principles

The project follows an agentic-AI-first development model. This means that AI agents — not just human developers — are primary contributors to the codebase. Human team members act as supervisors, reviewers, and strategic decision-makers, while AI agents handle implementation, testing, and documentation maintenance.

To enable this workflow, the repository contains structured instruction files that AI agents must read before performing any task:

  • .cursorrules / .antigravityrules — Master instruction files that enforce the pre-flight (read your role) and post-flight (update documentation) mandates.
  • .agents/ directory — Role-specific constraints for Product Manager, Architect, Developer, and QA Tester agents.
  • /docs/ directory — This documentation hub. Every page here should be treated as a living document. After any meaningful implementation, agents must update the relevant documentation pages.
⚠️ Documentation is never optional. Agents must include documentation updates in the same commit or pull request as any code change. A task is not considered complete until the relevant /docs/ pages reflect the current state of the codebase.

For the full agent workflow, role definitions, and rules, see Brainstorming Board.
